=== events 61 sparc project symposium point of care healthcare devices ===
Source: https://www.iitk.ac.in/gsmst/events/61-sparc-project-symposium-point-of-care-healthcare-devices
Published: 25 November 2024

SPARC Project Symposium on "Point-of-Care Healthcare Devices" & Pant Workshop Series on Medical Technology & Future Medicine GSMST, IIT Kanpur | November 25–27, 2024

Hosted by the Gangwal School of Medical Sciences and Technology (GSMST), IIT Kanpur, this three-day symposium brought together researchers from IIT Kanpur, the University of Melbourne, and Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham for interdisciplinary dialogue across biomedical sciences, engineering, and emerging health technologies. Sixteen scientific talks were delivered over two intensive lecture days, covering topics such as antimicrobial resistance, nanomaterial-based drug delivery, AI in biological sequence analysis, bone regeneration, mRNA Immunotherapeutics, structural biology, and collaborative robotics in dentistry. The event was co-organized by Prof. Sandeep Verma (IIT Kanpur), Prof. Debnath Ghosal, and Prof. Richard Strugnell (University of Melbourne).

Women’s Collective Forum (WCF)

Collaboration lies at the core of WCF’s work. From shaping policies to delivering scalable interventions, the Forum is a trusted partner in addressing systemic challenges with actionable solutions.

=== events 70 sparc symposium on india australia cooperation ===
Source: https://www.iitk.ac.in/gsmst/events/70-sparc-symposium-on-india-australia-cooperation
Published: 29 November 2024

SPARC Symposium on India-Australia Cooperation in Medical Education and Biomedical Research Melbourne Global Centre, New Delhi | November 29, 2024

Jointly organized by the Faculty of Medicine, Dentistry and Health Sciences (FMDHS), University of Melbourne, and the Graduate School of Medicine, Science and Technology (GSMST), IIT Kanpur, this one-day symposium explored two complementary themes. The first session addressed EdTech and AI in medical education, with presentations on assessment innovation, AI-driven learning tools, and advancing healthcare technologies. The second session, focused on translational research spanning cancer therapy, biomedical engineering, hysteroscopy simulation, novel bone-drilling techniques, and nanotechnology-based diagnostics, reflecting the breadth of the ongoing India-Australia research collaboration.

=== events 71 sparc international symposium 2026 ===
Source: https://www.iitk.ac.in/gsmst/events/71-sparc-international-symposium-2026
Published: 09 February 2026

SPARC International Symposium on Biotechnology and Clinical Research Amritapuri Campus | February 9–11, 2026

A three-day symposium was organized under the SPARC initiative, bringing together Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham, IIT Kanpur, and the University of Melbourne. The event covered four major technical tracks: molecular therapeutics and point-of-care diagnostics, AI integration in computational medicine, disease biology and cellular plasticity, and antimicrobial resistance (AMR) with mucosal immunity. Key themes included bench-to-bedside translation, bacteriophage therapy, predictive biomarkers, and AI-driven diagnostics. The symposium concluded with facility tours, poster sessions, and discussions on joint publications under the SPARC mandate.

=== events 72 sparc international workshop ===
Source: https://www.iitk.ac.in/gsmst/events/72-sparc-international-workshop
Published: 13 February 2026

Innovations in Translational Biomedical Research Workshop Shiv Nadar University, Delhi-NCR | February 13, 2026

A one-day workshop, co-organized by Shiv Nadar University, IIT Kanpur, and the University of Melbourne, focused on various scientific topics. Two sessions covered precision oncology, exosome-based drug delivery, biologics in public health, computational immunology, and chemical glycobiology. The workshop aimed to establish frameworks for joint SPARC Phase-II grant applications, PhD student exchange programs, and shared multi-omics data repositories. The event ended with discussions among institutional leaders on how to sustain long-term collaboration.

Professor Anil K.Lalwani, dean of its Gangwal School of Medical Sciences and Technology, told India Today Digital that the institute will launch higher and super-speciality medical degrees first: the MD, DM and MCh qualifications that turn a general physician into a cardiologist or a neurosurgeon, alongside transdisciplinary research programmes. === media coverage 84 iitk soon offer super speciality medical degrees ===
Source: https://www.iitk.ac.in/gsmst/media-coverage/84-iitk-soon-offer-super-speciality-medical-degrees
Published: 17 September 2026

Thursday, 17 September 2026, Publication: India Today, Edition: Online,

Source: Radifah Kabir

Summary: And yet at IIT Kanpur this year, on a stretch of land running down towards the Ganga, a hospital is rising in stages, cranes standing against a sky just beginning to clear after the monsoon, workers laying brick for wards that do not yet have a single patient inside them. Those wards will one day belong to doctors trained the way no doctors in India currently are, and the institute has just confirmed, for the first time on record, exactly which degrees will fill them first. Not the MBBS (Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery) most Indians grow up associating with becoming a doctor, but higher medical degrees, the kind pursued after basic medical training to become a specialist, alongside the super-speciality degrees known as DM (Doctor of Medicine) and MCh (Master of Chirurgiae, or Master of Surgery).
