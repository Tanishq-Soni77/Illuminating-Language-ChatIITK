Central Research Facilities
Central Research Facilities
CREATING SHARED INFRASTRUCTURE FOR ADVANCED DISCOVERY
Breakthrough research increasingly depends on access to highly specialised facilities for fabrication, imaging, spectroscopy and performance testing.
IIT Kanpur’s proposed Central Research Facilities will bring these capabilities together within a shared scientific ecosystem. The initiative will support researchers working across semiconductors, advanced materials, quantum technologies, energy, healthcare, electronics and other emerging fields.
By creating common access to sophisticated equipment and technical expertise, the facilities will enable researchers to move more efficiently from material development and device fabrication to detailed analysis, validation and application.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus priorities central research facilities]
ENABLING RESEARCH ACROSS THE COMPLETE DEVELOPMENT CYCLE
Advanced research rarely depends on a single instrument.
A new material may need to be fabricated, processed, imaged and tested before its potential can be understood. A semiconductor device may require controlled cleanroom conditions, thin-film growth, precision patterning and metrology.
A scientific discovery may need multiple forms of structural, compositional, magnetic, optical and mechanical analysis before it can progress towards a functional technology.
The Central Research Facilities will connect these stages within one integrated platform.
This shared model will reduce infrastructure duplication, strengthen interdisciplinary collaboration and give faculty, students and research teams access to capabilities that would be difficult to establish within individual departments.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus priorities central research facilities]
ABOUT THE CENTRAL RESEARCH
FACILITIES
THE INITIATIVE BRINGS TOGETHER SIX
SPECIALISED CENTRES:
01. Semiconductor and Device Processing Center
A shared platform for cleanroom-based research, thin-film growth, patterning, lithography, fabrication, metrology and device processing.
Together, these centres will provide a connected research pathway—from creating materials and devices to understanding their structure, behaviour and potential applications.
PHILANTHROPIC SUPPORT CAN HELP ENABLE:
Cleanroom and semiconductor-processing infrastructure
Thin-film growth and lithography facilities
Advanced materials fabrication
Structural and compositional spectroscopy
High-resolution and specialised microscopy
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus priorities central research facilities]
Device and mechanical characterisation
Technical expertise and specialist training
Long-term maintenance and technology upgrades

Gangwal School of Medical Sciences and Technology
Gangwal School of Medical
Sciences and Technology
REIMAGINING HEALTHCARE THROUGH THE CONVERGENCE OF MEDICINE AND TECHNOLOGY
The Gangwal School of Medical Sciences and Technology at IIT Kanpur is being established to create a new model for healthcare education, research and innovation in India.
The School will bring medical sciences together with IIT Kanpur’s strengths in engineering, artificial intelligence, robotics, materials science, imaging, diagnostics and data-driven research. Its purpose is to enable clinicians, scientists and engineers to work within one integrated ecosystem—developing technologies and models of care that respond to India’s most pressing healthcare challenges.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus priorities gangwal school]
Anchored by advanced biomedical research centres and a 500-bed super-specialty hospital, GSMST will connect scientific discovery with clinical practice. The ambition is not simply to create another medical institution, but to build an environment in which healthcare problems can be studied, solutions can be developed and innovations can be tested in real clinical settings.
FROM SCIENTIFIC DISCOVERY TO BETTER CARE
Healthcare innovation often remains fragmented. Engineers may develop promising technologies without sustained access to clinical environments. Medical professionals may identify urgent problems but lack the technical teams and research infrastructure required to develop new solutions. Even strong prototypes can struggle to progress through validation, regulation, manufacturing and adoption.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus priorities gangwal school]
GSMST is designed to reduce these distances.
By bringing clinical departments, biomedical research, technology development and patient care together, the School can create a continuous pathway from identifying a healthcare need to developing, validating and deploying a solution.
This integrated model will support innovation in areas such as medical devices, diagnostics, imaging, artificial intelligence, robotics, telemedicine, prosthetics, drug discovery and precision healthcare.
ABOUT THE GANGWAL
SCHOOL
GSMST WILL COMBINE THREE INTERCONNECTED DIMENSIONS:
Advanced Clinical Care
A 500-bed super-specialty hospital will provide advanced care while creating a clinical environment for education, research and technology validation. Proposed capabilities include specialised services in oncology, cardiology, neurosciences, gastroenterology, paediatrics, transplantation, diagnostics and surgical care.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus priorities gangwal school]
A NEW GENERATION OF HEALTHCARE LEADERS
The School will also help develop professionals who are fluent across the boundaries of medicine and technology. Future doctors will benefit from exposure to engineering, data and innovation. Engineers and scientists will gain a deeper understanding of clinical needs, patient outcomes and healthcare delivery.
This interdisciplinary culture can produce a new generation of clinicians, researchers, entrepreneurs and technology leaders capable of building healthcare systems that are more accessible, precise and responsive.
PHILANTHROPIC SUPPORT CAN HELP ESTABLISH:
Super-specialty hospital and clinical departments
Cancer care and advanced treatment facilities
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus priorities gangwal school]
Biomedical research centres and laboratories
Medical imaging, diagnostics and surgical infrastructure
Faculty chairs and research fellowships
Student scholarships and interdisciplinary education
Medical-device and diagnostic innovation
Technology validation and clinical translation
Partnerships with hospitals, industry and global institutions

Halls of Residence
Halls of Residence
CREATING BETTER PLACES TO LIVE, LEARN AND BELONG
IIT Kanpur’s residential campus is central to the student experience. Halls of Residence are not simply places where students stay; they are where friendships are formed, ideas are exchanged, communities are built and much of campus life unfolds beyond the classroom.
To support a growing student population, IIT Kanpur is expanding its residential infrastructure through four new Halls of Residence: Hall 15 for women students, and Halls 16, 17 and 18 for men students.
These new air-conditioned residences are being planned as modern, comfortable and sustainable living environments that can support academic focus, personal well-being and a strong sense of belonging.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus priorities halls of residence]
SUPPORTING A GROWING  STUDENT COMMUNITY
As IIT Kanpur expands its academic programmes and student intake, residential capacity must grow alongside it.
High-quality on-campus housing allows students to remain fully engaged with the life of the Institute. It reduces daily logistical pressures, supports access to academic and co-curricular activities, and creates opportunities for peer learning across disciplines and year groups.
A well-designed Hall of Residence can also contribute meaningfully to student well-being. Safe, comfortable and inclusive spaces help students adjust to campus life, develop independence and find the community support they need during demanding academic years.
By strengthening residential infrastructure, IIT Kanpur can preserve the immersive campus experience that has shaped generations of students.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus priorities halls of residence]

OF RESIDENCE

IOTA
IOTA: Interface Office for
Technology Applications
MOVING RESEARCH FROM THE LABORATORY TO THE REAL WORLD
The proposed Interface Office for Technology Applications, or IOTA, will strengthen IIT Kanpur’s ability to translate promising research into technologies that can be tested, adopted and deployed.
Many academic innovations demonstrate strong scientific potential but struggle to progress beyond the prototype stage. They may require further engineering, validation, industry access, development funding or a clearer understanding of market and user needs.
IOTA is being designed to bridge this gap. By supporting technologies as they mature from early-stage research towards market readiness, the Office will help connect IIT Kanpur’s scientific capability with industry, entrepreneurship and real-world application.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus priorities iota]
FROM RESEARCH POTENTIAL TO PRACTICAL APPLICATION
Technology translation is rarely a straight path.
A laboratory prototype may need to be redesigned for reliability, cost or scale. A researcher may require an industry partner to validate performance in real operating conditions. A promising technology may need regulatory guidance, specialised facilities or patient capital before it can progress.
IOTA will provide a structured interface through which these needs can be identified and addressed.
Its role will include supporting early-stage ideas, sourcing relevant industry problems, enabling collaborative development and helping technologies advance through higher levels of readiness. The larger objective is to ensure that more IIT Kanpur innovations have a credible pathway from discovery to deployment.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus priorities iota]
ABOUT IOTA
IOTA WILL WORK ACROSS STRATEGIC AREAS WHERE IIT KANPUR ALREADY HAS SIGNIFICANT RESEARCH AND INNOVATION CAPABILITY, INCLUDING:
MedTech
Quantum technologies
Semiconductors
Unmanned aerial systems
Sustainability, CleanTech and AgriTech
Cybersecurity
The Office is expected to direct a substantial share of its support towards early-stage technology development and help innovations progress from approximately TRL 4 towards TRL 9.
By bringing researchers, industry partners, funders and technology-development teams together, IOTA can help reduce the distance between academic research and market-ready solutions. It can also strengthen Kanpur and Uttar Pradesh as centres for technology-led enterprise and industrial innovation.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus priorities iota]

Early-stage technology development
Prototype refinement and validation
Industry-led research collaborations
Technology-readiness and deployment support
Translational talent and project teams
Market, regulatory and commercialisation pathways
Startup and licensing opportunities

A Family Gift That Keeps Ideas Moving
Rajeev Motwani Building
The Rajeev Motwani Building provides IIT Kanpur’s Department of Computer Science and Engineering with laboratories, classrooms and spaces for research and collaboration.
Created through a USD 1.5 million contribution from Asha Jadeja Motwani and her daughters, Naitri and Anya, the Rajeev Motwani Building honours one of IIT Kanpur’s most influential computer scientists. Today, it provides a permanent home for the research, collaboration and entrepreneurial thinking that defined his life.
When a Building Carries an Idea Forward
The most meaningful institutional buildings do more than create space. They carry forward a way of thinking.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories a family gift that keeps ideas moving]
At IIT Kanpur, the six-storey Rajeev Motwani Building houses the Department of Computer Science and Engineering while supporting research, teaching, collaboration and innovation.
It is both an academic facility and a living memorial—significant not only because of the name outside, but because of the intellectual activity continuing within.
Remembering a Life Through the Future
The building was made possible by Asha Jadeja Motwani, wife of the late Prof. Rajeev Motwani, together with their daughters.
On 24 March 2011, what would have been Rajeev’s 49th birthday, the family announced a USD 1.5 million contribution towards the new building. Its foundation stone was laid in May 2011, and the completed facility was inaugurated in December 2014.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories a family gift that keeps ideas moving]
Rather than create a static monument, the family chose to support a place where future generations could study, experiment and build.
From IIT Kanpur to Global Influence
Rajeev Motwani entered IIT Kanpur in 1978 as part of its first undergraduate Computer Science and Engineering batch.
After graduating in 1983, he earned his doctorate at the University of California, Berkeley, and later joined Stanford University. His research made foundational contributions to randomized algorithms, approximation algorithms, computational complexity, data mining and the mathematical understanding of the web. He received the Gödel Prize in 2001.
His influence extended beyond research. Rajeev was widely recognised as a mentor who identified potential early and helped ambitious ideas develop. He advised students, researchers and technology founders, while remaining closely connected with IIT Kanpur’s CSE research and entrepreneurship ecosystem.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories a family gift that keeps ideas moving]
Creating Room for a Growing Discipline
The building added approximately 3,650 square metres of academic space, including a 200-seat classroom, 22 faculty offices and 17 laboratories.
It gave the department greater capacity to work across theoretical computer science, artificial intelligence, machine learning, systems, cybersecurity, data science and emerging interdisciplinary areas.
Its original vision also extended to entrepreneurship. The building became part of a wider effort to help students and faculty translate ideas and technologies into ventures.
More Than a Name on a Building
Rajeev Motwani died unexpectedly in 2009 at the age of 47. His life was brief, but his intellectual influence was not. At IIT Kanpur, his name now belongs to a place where new computer scientists begin journeys of their own.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories a family gift that keeps ideas moving]
Every class taught, experiment conducted, student mentored and idea pursued within that space carries the legacy forward.

Shivani Centre
The Shivani Centre helps students navigate language barriers while strengthening learning, literature and cultural expression across the IIT Kanpur campus.
Established through a USD 1 million contribution from distinguished alumnus Muktesh “Micky” Pant and the Micky and Vinita Charitable Foundation, the Shivani Centre honours the legacy of Padma Shri author Gaura Pant ‘Shivani’. At IIT Kanpur, that literary inheritance supports a contemporary mission: ensuring that language does not become a barrier to learning, confidence or belonging.
Arriving with ability—and an unfamiliar language
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories a gift that helps every student belong]
Every student entering IIT Kanpur has demonstrated exceptional academic ability. Yet students do not all arrive equally prepared for an English-dominated academic environment.
For those educated primarily in Hindi or another Indian language, understanding technical vocabulary, following lectures and expressing complex ideas may require additional effort. A student may know the answer but hesitate to speak. Over time, a language gap can become a confidence gap, affecting participation and belonging.
The Shivani Centre for the Nurture and Reintegration of Hindi and Other Indian Languages helps students navigate this transition while recognising the knowledge and linguistic identity they already possess.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories a gift that helps every student belong]
Its purpose is not to lower academic standards or replace English. It is to remove an incidental barrier so that students can demonstrate more fully what they are capable of.
A son’s tribute to a literary voice
The Centre was established by Muktesh “Micky” Pant, a 1976 graduate of IIT Kanpur’s Department of Chemical Engineering, through the Micky and Vinita Charitable Foundation.
His gift honours his mother, Smt. Gaura Pant, known to generations of readers as Shivani. One of the twentieth century’s most widely read Hindi authors, she wrote extensively about human relationships, women’s inner lives and India’s social worlds, receiving the Padma Shri in 1982.
The connection is deeply fitting. A writer devoted her life to giving people and experiences expression through language. The institution bearing her name now helps ensure that students are not denied expression because of language.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories a gift that helps every student belong]
Making knowledge more accessible
The Centre supports students from diverse linguistic and educational backgrounds and works to expand academic material in Hindi and other Indian languages.
Faculty members, tutors and student volunteers create and translate learning resources. In one initiative, 20 National Service Scheme volunteers began producing audiovisual and translated content for important courses.
The Centre also explores how technology can make Indian languages easier to use across digital platforms—connecting its cultural mission with IIT Kanpur’s technological capabilities.
Inclusion works in both directions. Hindi classes for international students help them navigate campus life and engage more naturally with the surrounding community.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories a gift that helps every student belong]
Language, literature and institutional culture
Through initiatives such as the Akshar literature festival and Katha – The Art of Story Writing, the Centre brings writers, poets, performers, scholars and students together through literature, theatre, music and storytelling.
Micky Pant’s philanthropy therefore supports more than language instruction. It strengthens an institutional culture in which seeking support is not seen as inadequacy, linguistic identity does not need to disappear, and technical excellence can coexist with cultural understanding.
At IIT Kanpur, excellence brings students through the gates. The Shivani Centre helps ensure that language does not prevent them from finding their place once they arrive.

The Ranjit Singh Rozi Shiksha Kendra
The Ranjit Singh Rozi Shiksha Kendra connects rural children, youth and artisans with vocational skills and livelihood technologies.

At the Ranjit Singh Rozi Shiksha Kendra, IIT Kanpur’s knowledge travels beyond the campus—into rural classrooms, artisan communities and farming clusters—connecting education with skills, enterprise and the possibility of a more secure future.
The distance opportunity must travel
For a child in a rural school, access to a good science teacher may depend on geography. For a young person seeking work, opportunity may depend on acquiring a market-relevant skill. For a traditional artisan, generations of knowledge can be threatened by changing markets and limited visibility.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories a gift that takes learning further]
The challenges differ, but the divide is the same: talent and aspiration are widely distributed; access to knowledge, technology and opportunity is not.
Established at IIT Kanpur in 2021, the Ranjit Singh Rozi Shiksha Kendra works to bridge that divide through two connected goals—quality education for rural children and sustainable livelihoods for rural communities.
Taking learning beyond the campus
Through its Online Rural Education Initiative, IIT Kanpur scholars and volunteers teach Mathematics and Science to students in Classes 9 to 12 through interactive smart classrooms. Lessons follow the NCERT curriculum and are delivered in accessible, conversational Hindi.
During 2024-25, the programme reached 11 schools and delivered 966 live classes over 181 working days, supported by more than 50 volunteers.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories a gift that takes learning further]
Learning also extends beyond textbooks. Educational visits, electronics and Arduino workshops, astronomy sessions and portable science kits give students opportunities to experiment and build. A CSR-supported “Lab in a Box” provides more than 100 hands-on experiments to schools with limited laboratory infrastructure.
From inherited skill to sustainable enterprise
Through Kalagram, its Design Development Centre, the Kendra connects artisans, designers, technologists and rural entrepreneurs. In Bithoor, more than 100 potters have received design inputs, improved techniques, access to government programmes and platforms to showcase their work.
The same approach supports women working in sewing, embroidery and sustainable home furnishings, as well as artisans associated with Kalpi’s GI-tagged handmade-paper tradition.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories a gift that takes learning further]

Through a NABARD-supported initiative, farmers—particularly women—are trained to use solar dehydration to reduce post-harvest losses and create value-added products. Vocational programmes in leather stitching and sewing-machine operations enrolled 172 rural youth during 2024-25.
A legacy that keeps creating opportunity
The Kendra carries forward the vision of Late Dr. Ranjit Singh, a distinguished alumnus of IIT Kanpur’s pioneer batch. Through the Ranjit Singh Foundation, his philanthropy supported scholarships, an academic chair and the institutional platform that became the Kendra.
His contribution did more than fund a programme. It created a model through which education, technology, design and community partnership can work together over time.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories a gift that takes learning further]
Through the Kendra, IIT Kanpur is demonstrating that knowledge creates its greatest value when it travels—and when the opportunity it creates becomes greater than any one institution, donor or generation.

SIIC
IIT Kanpur’s Startup Incubation and Innovation Centre supports founders from ideation and prototyping through incubation, investment readiness and market access.
Long before India’s startup ecosystem became a national movement, IIT Kanpur began building a place where technical ideas could be tested against the realities of the market. Established in 2000, the Startup Incubation and Innovation Centre has grown into one of India’s most established technology incubators—helping researchers, students and entrepreneurs move from early concepts to products, companies and real-world impact.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories backing ideas on their journey to ente]
Bridging the distance from idea to enterprise
A promising technology is not yet a successful innovation.
A researcher may prove that an idea works in a laboratory. A student may build a compelling prototype. Yet the journey towards a sustainable enterprise requires much more: product refinement, market validation, intellectual-property guidance, funding, specialist talent and access to customers.
Many ideas fail not because the science is weak, but because the ecosystem around the innovator is incomplete. SIIC was created to close that gap.
Building before the ecosystem existed
Established in collaboration with the Small Industries Development Bank of India, SIIC began at a time when organised technology incubation in India was still emerging.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories backing ideas on their journey to ente]
By placing incubation within IIT Kanpur, it connected entrepreneurial ambition with laboratories, faculty expertise, research capabilities and the Institute’s problem-solving culture.
In 2018, its operations were strengthened under the Foundation for Innovation & Research in Science & Technology, a Section 8 company promoted by IIT Kanpur.
Today, SIIC supports ventures from ideation and prototyping to acceleration, investment readiness and market access.
More than a place to work
Technology startups need more than office space. SIIC combines infrastructure with technical and business mentoring, government-supported programmes, funding opportunities, investor engagement and access to IIT Kanpur’s wider research and alumni networks.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories backing ideas on their journey to ente]
Pre-incubation programmes help innovators test ideas. Longer-term incubation supports product and business-model development. Accelerators help selected ventures prepare for deployment, investment and scale.
The ecosystem spans sectors including aerospace, healthcare, agriculture, defence, artificial intelligence, clean technology, advanced manufacturing, biotechnology and cybersecurity.
From research to real-world value
In September 2025, SIIC crossed the milestone of 500 startups incubated. By December 2025, more than 230 ventures were presently incubated, over 50 were being onboarded and the ecosystem included 49 faculty entrepreneurs.
SIIC also helps IIT Kanpur research move beyond publication and proof of concept. Faculty members and researchers receive support in company formation, intellectual-property management, product development and commercialisation.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories backing ideas on their journey to ente]
This is particularly important for deep technology, where a medical device, advanced material, drone platform or agricultural machine may require years of refinement before reaching the market.
The next chapter
After more than 25 years, SIIC has established entrepreneurship as a core expression of IIT Kanpur’s problem-solving culture.
Its next phase will require deeper technical infrastructure, patient capital, stronger market connections and expanded support for ventures addressing national priorities.
The most visible output of an incubator is a startup. Its deeper contribution is the confidence it gives people to act on knowledge—helping ideas travel from laboratories and classrooms into the lives of the people they were created to serve.

Backing Intelligence Built for the Real World
Wadhwani School
The Wadhwani School of AI & Intelligent Systems integrates AI, robotics, cybersecurity, cyber-physical systems, policy and technology translation.
The Wadhwani School of AI & Intelligent Systems represents IIT Kanpur’s effort to move artificial intelligence beyond algorithms and isolated applications. Established with the support of the Wadhwani Foundation, the School brings together AI, robotics, cybersecurity, cyber-physical systems, policy and technology translation to create intelligent systems that can operate securely, responsibly and at scale.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories backing intelligence built for the rea]
Intelligence Must Engage With the Real World
Artificial intelligence is often understood through software. Its larger impact will emerge when intelligence becomes part of the physical and institutional systems on which society depends.
An autonomous machine must respond safely to changing environments. A digital system managing critical infrastructure must remain secure under attack. An AI tool used in healthcare, finance or governance must be accurate, explainable and aligned with the people affected by its decisions.
These challenges require computing, engineering, hardware, control systems, cybersecurity and policy to work together.
WSAIS was created around this wider understanding—not simply as an expansion of computer-science education, but as a multidisciplinary institution connecting foundational research with responsible real-world deployment.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories backing intelligence built for the rea]
A Partnership Designed for Long-Term Capability
The School was established through a partnership between IIT Kanpur and the Wadhwani Foundation, formalised in April 2025.
The Foundation’s support extends beyond physical infrastructure to academic programmes, scholarships, research, translation and entrepreneurial pathways.
WSAIS will also serve as a national SuperHub within the Wadhwani Innovation Network, supporting a wider ecosystem of partner institutions and helping strengthen research and innovation capacity beyond IIT Kanpur.
From AI Models to Intelligent Systems
An AI model may perform well in controlled conditions and still fail during deployment. Data changes, sensors produce incomplete information, machines encounter unexpected situations and systems face cyber threats.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories backing intelligence built for the rea]
An intelligent system must therefore combine learning and reasoning with hardware, security, control and human oversight.
The School brings together advanced AI, robotics, cybersecurity, cyber-physical systems, policy, outreach and technology translation. This allows discoveries to move from theory to prototype, from prototype to secure system and eventually towards responsible application.
Building for India’s Priorities
India’s intelligent systems must work across diverse languages, geographies, infrastructure and levels of digital access.

Investing in a More Sustainable Future
Kotak School of Sustainability
The Kotak School of Sustainability brings engineering, science, policy and economics together to address climate, energy, water and resource challenges.
The Kotak School of Sustainability represents IIT Kanpur’s effort to build a new institutional model for one of the defining challenges of our time. Established with the support of Kotak Mahindra Bank through its CSR programme, the School brings science, engineering, economics, policy and society together to help India grow while becoming cleaner, more resilient and more resource-efficient.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories investing in a more sustainable future]
Sustainability Is a Systems Challenge
A Partnership Built Around a National Priority
Kotak Mahindra Bank’s support gave institutional form to an idea whose relevance extends far beyond IIT Kanpur.
Rather than fund a limited intervention, the partnership creates enduring capacity through faculty, researchers, academic programmes, collaborative centres and engagement with industry, government and society.
The School’s work spans clean energy, water, climate and ecology, sustainable infrastructure, circular economy, climate finance, economics, policy and artificial intelligence for sustainability.
This integrated approach allows researchers to ask not only whether a technology works, but whether it can be adopted, financed, governed and sustained in the real world.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories investing in a more sustainable future]
Beginning With India’s Realities
India must expand infrastructure, energy access and economic opportunity while responding to climate risks and long-term sustainability commitments.
Solutions must work across dense cities, industrial regions, agricultural communities and areas with very different levels of resources.
Located within IIT Kanpur, the School can combine scientific and engineering capability with economics, policy and social understanding to develop responses shaped around these realities.
Educating Leaders Across Boundaries
The transition to a sustainable economy will require professionals who can work across disciplines.
Engineers will need to understand regulation and environmental consequences. Policymakers will need greater fluency in technology and data. Business leaders will need to treat climate risk and resource efficiency as strategic concerns.
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories investing in a more sustainable future]
The School’s academic programmes, including its M.Tech. in Artificial Intelligence for Sustainability, are designed to develop this cross-disciplinary capacity.
A Living Laboratory
The School’s upcoming academic building is envisioned as a LEED Platinum-certified, Net Zero Energy facility using energy-efficient systems and environmentally sensitive materials.
Beyond its architecture, it can serve as a living laboratory where students and researchers study sustainable materials, energy systems, water management and building performance in practice.
Kotak Mahindra Bank’s founding support has enabled IIT Kanpur to create an institutional platform around a defining question:
How can societies continue to develop without compromising the systems on which future development depends?
[Context: IIT KANPUR - GREATER THAN US (GTU) FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGN: PRIORITIES AND STORIES > greaterthanus stories investing in a more sustainable future]
The Kotak School has been created to help find those answers.
